# ScrollNah privacy policy

_Last updated: 6 November 2026_

ScrollNah is an Android app that helps you spend less time on the apps you choose, with a friendly mascot (Blobby or Bloomy). This policy explains what the app looks at, what it keeps, and where it keeps it.

**The short version: everything stays on your phone. ScrollNah has no account, no servers, no ads and no analytics. It does not collect, sell or share your data with anyone.**

## What ScrollNah reads on your phone

To work, ScrollNah reads a few things on your device. None of it ever leaves the phone.

- **App usage (Usage access).** With your permission, ScrollNah reads Android's app usage statistics to see which app is in front and how many minutes you spent in the apps you chose to limit. This is how it counts your minutes, shows the Pause screen at your limit and reminds you to take breaks.
- **Your list of installed apps.** So you can pick which apps to limit. ScrollNah only shows apps that have a launcher icon.
- **Notifications, "Display over other apps" and running in the background.** These permissions let ScrollNah show reminders, the Pause screen and the mascot bubble at the right moment. They do not let ScrollNah read the content of other apps.

### Parent lock (optional, off by default)

If a parent turns on **Parent lock** (for a child's phone), ScrollNah also checks, on the phone only:

- whether the settings it needs (Usage access, "Display over other apps", notifications, running in the background) are still on,
- whether ScrollNah was force-stopped (Android tells an app this when it starts again),
- whether the phone's clock was moved by more than a couple of minutes.

It notes these in a short report that only someone with the parent PIN or pattern can open in ScrollNah's Settings. **Uninstall protection** (optional) makes ScrollNah a "device admin app" that asks for **no device powers at all**: Android simply asks to turn it off before ScrollNah can be uninstalled. ScrollNah cannot lock, wipe or control the phone.

### Optional Reels, Shorts and website tracker (off by default)

ScrollNah includes an optional tracker that uses Android's Accessibility service. It is **switched off by default**, and ScrollNah explains what it reads and asks you to agree before sending you to Android's settings to turn it on. If you turn it on, it only checks which screen is open in Instagram and YouTube (to spot Reels and Shorts), notices when their videos scroll (to count about how many Reels and Shorts you watch), and reads your browser's address bar (to count time on the websites of apps you limit, like youtube.com). It uses this only for your Reels, Shorts and website limits, the reel count and your weekly report. When you reach a Reels or Shorts limit you set (in Firm or Beast mode), it presses Back once to leave that screen, and that is the only thing it does on its own. It saves **only minutes and that count**, on your phone, never what is on the screen, which videos you watched or which pages you visited, and it sends nothing anywhere. It never reads messages, passwords or other apps. You can turn it off any time in Android's Accessibility settings.

## What ScrollNah stores

All of this is stored only on your phone, inside the app's private storage:

- The apps you limit, your daily limits and open limits, shared budgets, strictness, bedtime, work hours and break reminder settings
- Your "Do this instead" ideas for the Pause screen, and which hat or pot colour your mascot wears
- Daily minute totals, your streak and Bloomy's garden
- For the weekly report: per day, the minutes and number of visits for each app you limit, the time of your first use, and how many minutes fell in each hour of the day
- If the optional tracker is on: per day, the minutes in Reels, Shorts and limited websites, and about how many Reels and Shorts videos you scrolled (just the number)
- Your answers on the Pause screen (for example "Bored") and whether you took a break, used for your weekly report
- Your name, if you type one, so the app can say hello
- Your mascot and light/dark choice
- If Parent lock is on: a scrambled, salted fingerprint (hash) of the PIN or pattern, never the PIN itself, and the lock's report (up to 100 short entries such as "Display over other apps was turned off, Today 4:10 PM")

## What ScrollNah shares

**Nothing.** ScrollNah does not send any data to the developer or to anyone else. The app has no internet features of its own.

If you tap **Share** on a "week in review" card, ScrollNah makes a picture of that card and opens Android's share menu. The picture goes only to the app you pick there, and only because you chose to share it.

If you tap **Settings > Data > Save a backup**, ScrollNah writes one file with your limits, settings, history, streak and garden to the place you choose (for example your Downloads folder or a cloud drive app you pick). ScrollNah does not upload it anywhere itself. Anyone who has that file can read your usage history, so keep it somewhere private. **Restore a backup** only reads a file you pick.

The only link out of the app is **Settings > Feedback**, which opens a GitHub Discussions page in your browser with your message filled in, plus your app version, Android version and phone model to help with bugs. Nothing is sent unless you choose to post it on GitHub yourself, where GitHub's own privacy policy applies.

## Deleting your data

- **Settings > Data > Clear history** deletes your saved minutes, weekly report history, streak days, Pause answers and break history (your limits and garden stay).
- **Turning off Parent lock** (Settings > Parent lock) deletes the PIN fingerprint and the lock's report, and switches off uninstall protection.
- **Uninstalling ScrollNah** deletes everything it stored on the phone. If uninstall protection is on, Android asks to turn it off first. Backup files you saved stay where you put them; delete them like any other file.

## Children

ScrollNah is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect any personal information from anyone. Parents may set it up on a child's phone with Parent lock; even then, ScrollNah collects nothing and everything stays on that phone.
